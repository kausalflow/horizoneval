How to read these tables

Every number is skill in nats over repeating the last value: log(MSE of repeating the last value / MSE). Higher is better, 0 means no better than repeating the last value, negative means worse. x nats means a squared error lower by 1 - e^-x (column *_pct) or e^x times smaller: 0.1 nats is about 10% lower, 0.7 about 50% lower (2 times smaller), 2.3 about 90% lower (10 times smaller), 4.6 about 99% lower (100 times smaller).

The gains add up: total = F + L + beyond_linear. F is what the best simple rule gets (drift, seasonal naive, the mean, ... chosen per series on the history). L is what a linear model fitted on the history adds on top of that rule. beyond_linear is what the model adds on top of the linear model. F and L describe the data, not any model; beyond_linear and total belong to the model.

group is the forecast step: 1 is one step ahead, 2-4 and 5+ further ahead. The row 'all' pools squared errors over every step, so it is dominated by the far steps, where errors are largest; it is not the average of the other rows.

*_se is the standard error over series: a value within about two standard errors of 0 is not distinguishable from 0. saturated says whether giving the linear model more memory had stopped helping it on the history; if not, L is a lower bound.

verdict, per group: 'beyond linear' (the model beats the linear model by more than twice the standard error and more than the offset a fitted linear model leaves, with a saturated reference), 'no better than linear' (it does not beat it at all), 'not shown' (ahead, but not by enough), 'reference not saturated'. credited is the overall answer: 'beyond linear' only if every group says so.
